ZZepto RCA Problem framing

Closing the average order value gap

Zepto sits ₹157 behind the category leader on average order value. The gap is not demand and it is not brand. It is a stretch of the price range in which the product gives a shopper no reason at all to add one more thing.

The problem

Speed is the moat, and also the ceiling

Quick commerce economics are unforgiving. Every order carries a fixed cost in picking, packing and rider time, so a platform delivering a ₹540 basket in ten minutes earns less per delivery than one moving ₹697. Zepto's average order value has stayed flat while both competitors grew.

Home, search and cart are all built for speed to checkout rather than basket to completion. Once a shopper finds the item they came for, nothing in the product asks them to continue, so the session ends exactly where the urgent need ended.

QAre we growing average order value, or growing revenue?
AAverage order value, without losing order volume. Raising the basket by pushing away small urgent orders reads as a win on the headline number and a loss on the business, so order volume becomes a guardrail rather than a target.
QIs the ten minute promise negotiable?
ANo, in which case every proposal has to fit inside it. A bigger basket takes longer to pick, so delivery time becomes a second guardrail rather than an acceptable trade.

The approach · Market and user research

What we did to find the answer

Two inputs. First, a read of what the competition publishes, because in this category they say a great deal. Instamart attributes a 14% quarterly rise in average order value directly to Maxxsaver, its basket building mechanic, which is close to a stated answer. Second, a primary survey of 30 shoppers across Pune, Bengaluru, Chennai and Mumbai, which returned a midpoint basket of ₹414 and confirmed the gap is structural rather than seasonal.

The survey agreed on three things. The free delivery line is treated as a finish line, with 63% stopping at or near it and 40% stopping after a single item. Discovery is too generic to pull anyone sideways, with 43% saying they rarely try new categories. And the forgotten item only surfaces after payment, at which point the product has already closed the door.

Clustering the responses produced five behavioural segments, of which three are already responsive to basket building mechanics, together 67% of the sample. The read worth keeping is that the highest value segment does almost no discovery beyond the products it already buys, so the ceiling on Zepto's best shoppers is set entirely by discovery rather than by loyalty.

The diagnosis

Plotting the incentive structure against where orders actually land makes the problem obvious. Zepto rewards a shopper at ₹149 and then again at ₹1,000, and does nothing at all in between.

The reframe

The right question is not "how do we make shoppers browse for longer", because that attacks the moat. It is "how do we deepen baskets without breaking the ten minute promise", which is a question the product can actually answer.

The solutions

Five mechanics, each removing one blocker

MechanicWhat it doesBlocker it removes
Basket ladder Four visible slabs at ₹149, ₹299, ₹499 and ₹799, so a next step always exists. Collapsed for urgent sessions and expanded for browse sessions. The ₹149 to ₹1,000 dead zone
Pre cart discovery Intent routed cards on home, category and product pages. Recipe completion, adjacent category prompts, and a forgotten item widget. Discovery too generic to pull anyone sideways
Cart layer Three transparent nudges in the cart: an always on value line, real event based scarcity, and a standard offers lane. The cart behaves like a receipt, not a shelf
Post cart recovery A 60 to 120 second window after checkout asking whether anything was forgotten, with one tap adds while the delivery slot is still held. No way back in after payment
Intent ranked pairs Frequently bought together pairs on product and cart pages, ranked by current intent rather than by overall popularity. Rewards attach to items, never to the basket

The basket ladder ships first, and not because it scores well. It is the layer every other mechanic points at. Discovery, cart nudges and recovery all need a visible next step to exist before they have anything to say.

Design constraint

No dark patterns and no fabricated urgency. Scarcity messaging is only used where a real event backs it, because a quick commerce shopper who catches the product lying once has very little switching cost.

The prototype

What the app looks like once this ships

The same cart, the same two items, the same ₹312. The only thing that changes is whether the screen gives the shopper somewhere to go next.

Open prototype

How we would know

Three numbers, three guardrails

Segment targeted exposure across three cities with a matched in segment control rather than a blanket split, because baskets range from ₹311 to ₹575 across the five segments and random assignment would let segment mix masquerade as a treatment effect.

The test is powered to detect a ₹20 lift, ships on a ₹30 lift with no guardrail breach, and is killed by two consecutive guardrail failures or a negative primary metric at day 14. The phase one target is a population average order value of ₹575 to ₹595.

Takeaway

Protect the ten minute moat, but give the shopper a reason to keep building once the first need is solved. The gap to the category leader is not a discount the product forgot to offer. It is 850 rupees of silence in the middle of the price range, and the fix is simply to put something there.
